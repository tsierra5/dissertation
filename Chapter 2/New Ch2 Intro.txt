#Introduction
	In Chapter One, I gave a provisional, working definition of language revitalization- one that would be sufficient to explain why considerations of speaker autonomy are not enough to motivate this kind of undertaking, even defined as minimally as it has been so far. For the previous purpose, a very minimal definition of language revitalization is sufficient. In this chapter, however, part of my goal is developing a conception of language revitalization which has more robust conditions, in terms of its purpose and its outcomes. Specifically, the substance I wish to impart and develop in my conception is that revitalization occurs when language learners, by their learning, also come to metabolize and process specific forms of alienation and grief. Hence, there is a therapeutic good to be gained by those who engage in language revitalization as I articulate it here, provided they bear the right sort of relationship to the language being revitalized.
	I will arrive at this substantive conception of revitalization by first walking through several different cases of endangered language learning, and considering how each of these cases can be taken to deepen our understanding of what language revitalization is. Some of the cases I will consider are actual real-life cases, and some are abstract to the point of being hypothetical. Still, each case will help to put some new contours on the minimal understanding of revitalization that serves as the starting place. The cases I will walk through in this chapter will all serve to update the minimal conception of revitalization from being underspecified in terms of which individuals are to become endangered language-learners and what their relationship to the learned language is like.
	From some of the cases I discuss here, it will be clear that I write within the context of North American Indigeneity and the historical and existing impacts of US colonization and policies of cultural assimilation. While in certain places my remarks may prove inextricable from that context, I also take much of my thinking to be applicable to indigenous language revitalization in other places in the world, and to language revitalization writ large.
