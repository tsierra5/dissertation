The next problem with using the autonomy-based account to justify revitalization is related to, but slightly broader than, the first. 

The problem is that it only seems to tell us what an agent stands to lose if her language disappears; it does not tell us what she might gain from learning a new language. Thus the view prescribes that we prevent languages from falling out of use, but it does not directly give us any reason to try to reverse that trend and grow languages back, so to speak. 

These problems hopefully show the need for an answer to the why-question of revitalization that will motivate second-language learning and therefore reversal of language decline. I will now offer my own account, which says that the answer depends on where one is standing.
