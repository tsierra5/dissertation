In taking a philosophical approach to language revitalization, there are two general questions we can ask: "What does it mean to revitalize a language?" and "Why is it good to revitalize languages?" Call these the what-question and the why-question, respectively.

In this chapter I will try make progress with the why-question. I will do this by first examining one account of the value of languages that I believe does not succeed at answering the why-question. I call this account the autonomy-based account, which is due to Nowak (2019). I discuss two main reasons why the autonomy-based account does not succeed; first, [reason 1] and secondly, [reason 2]. I suggest that these issues with the autonomy-based account are symptomatic of the fact that it assumes a loaded picture of linguistic agency, one which centers speakers as the primary linguistic agents. I take this to indicate that a more balanced conception of linguistic agency, which holds that hearers are required for the realization of linguistic agency, is needed in order to make sense of the value of linguistic diversity and language reclamation.
