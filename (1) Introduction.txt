In taking a philosophical approach to language revitalization, there are two general questions we can ask: "What does it mean to revitalize a language?" and "Why is it good to revitalize languages?" Call these the what-question and the why-question, respectively.

In this chapter I will try make progress with the why-question. I will do this by first examining one account of the value of languages that I believe does not succeed at answering the why-question, and explaining two reasons why it does not succeed. Then I develop my own account which describes two main strains of answers to the why-question.
