	The resurrection case is the most extreme of revitalization scenarios. In the abstract, it might seem rather out of touch to even consider this kind of case. In chapter two, I will offer some considerations, however, that aim to concretize this scenario as one where compelling reasons for "resurrection" exist in certain contexts.
	At the very least, we will need to reach for some other tools than purely the ones we have tried to make use of so far in order to motivate, and perhaps even to justify, a devoted revitalization of extinct languages, as these seem to be a special case that is not properly touched by Nowak's argument for language preservation. On one hand this is understandable, because we have seen that he is primarily interested to center language protections on the interests of living individuals, insofar as the argument hinges on a basic shared understanding that we should not deprive people of autonomy, all things considered.However, I think it is entirely possible to leverage the interests of living individuals to argue for the good of revitalizing extinct languages, and by extension those which are not extinct either. I will develop my own account in attempt to do just that in the next chapter.
