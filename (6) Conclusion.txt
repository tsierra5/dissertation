To close, let me review: the concern of this paper has been to make progress on finding a suitable answer to the question of why we should revitalize languages. 

I first considered Nowak's view, on which the value of a language is tied to its role as an enabling condition of autonomy in the domain of social identity. I worked to show two related issues with Nowak's account, namely that it does not motivate learning new languages, which is an integral part of language revitalization, and therefore it can only serve to motivate the prevention of language decline rather than reversal. 

I then tried my hand at answering the why-question by developing a "two-nest" account on which languages can be valued in terms of their place in a global picture of language diversity, or they can be valued for the particular significance they hold for their communities. Lastly, I developed the latter strain of answers in more detail with two personal stories. These stories illustrate what learning the Cheyenne language means to me: creating and maintaining connection with my ancestors.
